Team, Costs and Limits

Who does the work, what it costs, and what we cannot do (yet).

The team is mostly AI agents

One boss plans the work and checks it. Helpers do the building. Other helpers check the building, without seeing how it was done. The owner (you) makes the big choices.

One work cycle

  1. Plan. The controller writes a plan with exact tests. Fable reviews it once.
  2. Brief. Each task gets a brief: context, goals, non-goals, how it will be checked.
  3. Build. An implementer works in its own copy of the repo (a git worktree).
  4. Review. A fresh reviewer checks it blind. Up to a few fix rounds.
  5. Merge and verify. The controller merges and re-runs everything.
  6. Ledger. Every step, ruling and number goes in the log, so work can resume after a pause.

Our limits

  • Law. The game and its art belong to LEGO. We ship only our own code. Players bring their own files. The README screenshots need a decision before the repo goes public.
  • No running original. We never run the .exe. So we cannot record the true game directly. Our oracles are rebuilt from reading.
  • Gameplay has a weak oracle. Physics, AI and camera are harder to prove than file reading. Milestone M3 will need recordings or screenshots from you.
  • Human eyes. Visual work needs your screenshots of the real menu and areas. Tests cannot judge "looks right".
  • Budget and quota. Credits are finite. The Max plan's weekly limit is shared with other projects.
  • Browsers. WebGPU support differs. Safari and Firefox are not tested yet.
  • Performance. Clean speed numbers need a quiet Mac (load under 6).

Lessons we keep

  • Never say "it works" without running it. Mark each claim verified or not.
  • Decompiler borders are hints. Take function ends from the bytes.
  • Two engines that agree can still both be wrong.
  • Long waits run in the background. No step blocks for more than two minutes.
  • Measure cost per task, then choose the model. Do not guess.

Check yourself

Why do reviewers work blind?

If a reviewer sees the author's reasoning, they tend to accept it. A blind reviewer reads the binary fresh and catches shared mistakes.

Why not use the strongest model for everything?

Cost. Our test showed Haiku finds code as well as Opus at about a tenth of the price. Opus stays where judgment matters.

What does the project need from you before M2 closes?

Screenshots of the real main menu and Options screen, and approval for any push, merge to main or public release.
